Recently online intelligent education has caught more and more attention, especially due
to the global inﬂuence of Covid-19. A major task of intelligent education is Knowledge
Tracing (KT) which aims to capture students’ dynamic status based on their historical inter-

1. Introduction
With the rapid development of adaptive learning, intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) [1] have been widely implemented.
Among research on ITS, more and more attention has been paid to modeling the knowledge state of each student intelli-
gently [2]. As an emerging paradigm of ITS, knowledge tracing (KT) [3] aims to accurately infer the latent state of students
based on their historical interactions and predict their responses to a new question. According to the predicted results, teach-
ers can know the mastery of students’ knowledge levels in advance and personalize learning plans for each student.
KT methods can date back to the late 1960s. In the early research, the hidden Markov chain was taken to extract the state
of students. Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) [4] is one of typical KT methods. Later, some KT models were built upon Item
Response Theory (IRT) [5], like the Difﬁculty, Ability, and Student History (DASH) model [6]. DASH innovatively takes into

However, the accuracy of these traditional KT methods strongly depends on the statistical estimation of parameters, and may
be impracticable nowadays.
Over the past decade, there has emerged a myriad of attempts that implement neural networks to KT. Due to the strong
capability of modeling sequential data, neural network-based KT methods generally perform better than traditional ones. For
example, Deep Knowledge Tracing (DKT) [7] ﬁrst introduces recurrent neural network (RNN) [8] to track the state evolution
process of students. But this standard sequential architecture of DKT is incapbale of both capturing long-term dependencies
and preserving semantic details. To resolve this issue, some KT methods based on memory neural networks, such as Dynamic
Key-Value Memory Networks for Knowledge Tracing (DKVMN) [9], were proposed, which supplement an external memory
matrix to store the dynamic change of the knowledge state of students.
Later, attention mechanism [10] gradually achieves promising results in NLP ﬁeld and is implemente to KT. Those
attention-based KT models eliminate irrelevant interactions and focus on relevant ones to predict a student’s next response.
Among them, the Self-Attentive Knowledge Tracing (SAKT) [11] utilizes the attention mechanism to learn the weight of each
historical interaction for the target question. Although SAKT shows its effectiveness in capturing early details, it completely
ignores the forgetting effects of the memory. Due to the long record cycle in KT, directly applying the attention mechanism
may result in signiﬁcant performance degeneration. In light of this issue, Context-Aware Attentive Knowledge Tracing (AKT)
[12] introduces a monotonic attention mechanism that integrates the exponential decay and characterizes the importance of
memory decay in modeling long interaction sequences.
Recently, Graph Neural Network (GNN) has emerged as a powerful strategy for analyzing graph-structured data and
found many applications in KT. GNN can sufﬁciently learn node embeddings by aggregating graph attributive and structural
features. In the online tutoring systems, KT usually encounters the cold-start or long-tail problem, i.e., most questions are
only interacted by a few students. How to tackle such problem and build better representation for questions has attracted
much attention. A Graph-based Interaction Model for Knowledge Tracing (GIKT) [13] treats the question-skill data as pair-
wise relations and builds the corresponding graph. A joint graph convolutional network based Deep Knowledge Tracing (JKT)
[14] models the multi-dimensional relationships of ‘‘exercise-to-exercise” and ‘‘concept-to-concept” into graph. Pre-training
Question Embeddings via Relation Map for Improving Knowledge Tracing (PERM) [15] innovatively exploits two relation
maps between questions, including co-occurrence and answer-agreement, and leverages a two-level attention mechanism
to fuse multi-faceted information. However, most GNN-based KT methods are susceptible to the subjective tendency of
experts within the Q-matrix. To resolve this issue, tracking knowledge proﬁciency of students with calibrated Q-matrix
(CQKT) [16] devises a novel Calibrated Q-matrix-based framework and achieves the state-of-the-art performance. In sum-
mary, the graph structure can effectively capture the latent relations among questions and enrich the representation of ques-
tions to some extent.
Although GNN-based methods have achieved signiﬁcant progresses in KT, they still suffer from the following limitations:
(1) The complex heterogeneity is neglected. Heterogeneous information network (HIN) [17] is ubiquitous in the real
world. In KT, these recorded questions are usually connected with diverse attributes, like question type, difﬁculty coef-
ﬁcient, involved skill, which are indispensable to build expressive representations for questions. Obviously, simple
homogeneous graphs (question-skill pair) are incapable of characterizing heterogeneous relations.
(2) The high-order relations are neglected. The features encoded just by pair-wise dependencies are not enough to capture
higher-order correlations among questions. In reality, multiple questions may cover a common skill, whose relations
are no longer dyadic, but higher-order. Traditional GNN can capture k-hop neighbor features by k layer propagation,
but is unable to represent the beyond-pairwise semantic structures and patterns.
To address the above limitations, we propose a novel knowledge tracing model and build question embeddings based on the
hypergraph [18], which can effectively capture the high-order relations among questions. Those questions, which share a
common skill, can be treated as the nodes connected by a hyperedge, and each skill is represented as a hyperedge. Further-
more, we propose a novel intra-graph attention to aggregate neighbor information of the hypergraph. Different from tradi-
tional convolution methods [19], which only perform a simple average-pooling over neighbor nodes, our proposed attention
can perceive the discrepancy in the impact of nodes and hyperedges and focus on important semantic features. Besides, to
quantify the importance of different nodes in the hypergraph, we design a novel projection function, which considers the
dynamic deviation under the impact of neighbors.
Although there have been much research on hypergraph learning, little of them has been done on the aforementioned
heterogeneity of real scenarios. To resolve this issue, we introduce a heterogeneous information network (HIN) into the
hypergraph and learn high-order and heterogeneous relations for question representations simultaneously. It is non-
trivial to bridge hypergraph and HIN. The hyperedges of the heterogeneous hypergraph are no longer simplex, but multiple.
Each hyperedge denotes a speciﬁc relation among questions. To learn the importance of different relations, we propose an
inter-graph attention to fuse multiple semantics for representations.
Currently, most KT methods are based on a supervised learning paradigm. However, the performance of those methods
may usually be degraded by the data sparsity, i.e., with millions of questions in the database, the observed student-question
interactions just provide feedback for a very small set of them, making it very difﬁcult to learn quality representations. To
address this limitation, we introduce self-supervised learning (SSL) into our model, which can ﬁnd supervised signals from

effectiveness of our proposed framework. Furthermore, our framework only involves upstream representation learning so
that it can be well adapted to various downstream prediction models. Our contributions can be summarized as follows.
1. We propose a novel heterogeneous hypergraph network (HHN) for representation learning, which can capture the
heterogeneous and high-order relations among questions. Moreover, with the proposed attentive mechanism, including
intra- and inter-graph attentions, our HHN can perceive the discrepancy in the impact of nodes and hyperedges, and con-
sequently focus on important connective structures.
2. We introduce a self-supervised task with adaptive augmentation to supplement auxiliary signals for heterogeneous
hypergraph modeling, aiming to learn intrinsic patterns and enhance robustness to perturbation.
3. We conduct extensive experiments on several real-world datasets and the results demonstrate our model’s superiority
compared with some state-of-the-art models.
2. Related work
2.1. Knowledge Tracing
In recent years, deep neural network (DNN) has found more and more applications in diverse ﬁelds, including knowledge
tracing (KT). KT aims to capture a student’s knowledge state according to his interactive questions and predict his response
to a new question. DKT [7], the ﬁrst attempt to employ RNN-based methods in KT, utilizes the LSTM network to model the
interactive questions sequences. DKVMN [9] leverages the memory-augmented network to retain long-term dependencies in
historical records. Additionally, the attention-based methods, like SAKT [11] and AKT [12], introduce attention mechanisms
to highlight these historical interactions that are highly relevant to the target question. ECD [22] proposes an educational
context-aware cognitive diagnosis framework to learn the cognitive states from the concerned educational context and stu-
dents’ historical learning records simultaneously. ABKT [23] introduces the ability factor into the learning feedback attribu-
tion and analyze the learning process from knowledge and ability. HawkesKT [24] investigates ﬁne-grained temporal cross-
effects between different skills in KT and explicitly models these cross-effects by the point process. SEEP [25] fuses questions
and concepts to obtain semantic-enhanced question embeddings. GIKT [13] builds a relation graph to extract the underlying
relations between questions and skills. PERM [15] exploits two relation maps from two perspectives of co-occurrence and
answer-agreement. CQKT [16] proposes a calibrated Q-matrix-based framework to dynamically track knowledge proﬁciency
of students.
Although those recurrent and attentive models have shown their superiority of modeling the sequential data in KT, they
all suffer from the inherent data sparsity problem, which makes it insufﬁcient to learn quality representations for a student’s
historical interactions. Although the GNN-based models can alleviate the sparsity problem to some extent, they may still fail
to extract the complex correlations among questions.
2.2. Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network
Recently, the heterogeneous graph has caught much research attention, and shown its great capacity of modeling various
types of nodes and relations. The meta-paths in HIN usually represent semantic relations between node pairs. By aggregating
features from different meta-paths, the heterogeneous structure can learn more comprehensive presentations for nodes than
a homogeneous graph. For example, HAN [26] utilizes hierarchical attention to capture the node-level and semantic-level
semantics. Based on HAN, DisenHAN [27] proposes a disentangled heterogeneous graph attention network for recommen-
dation, which considers the entanglement of latent factors stemming from different meta-paths. HetGNN [28] uses an LSTM
network to aggregate feature information of sampled neighboring nodes to obtain the ultimate node embeddings. Although
the above methods are capable of exploiting multi-faceted semantics, they neglect the beyond-pairwise relations in real
scenarios.
2.3. Hypergraph Learning
Beneﬁted from the power of hypergraph in extracting high-order relations, hypergraph learning has achieved great suc-
cesses in many ﬁelds. HGNN [19] is the ﬁrst method to apply graph convolution to aggregate neighbor features upon the

2.4. Contrastive Learning
As an emerging paradigm of self-supervised learning, contrastive learning aims to learn discriminative representations by
contrasting positive and negative pairs. It was ﬁrstly used in visual representation learning, in which negative samples are
generated by cropping, resizing, rotation, etc. Later, contrastive framework is extended to enhance graph representation
learning via self-discrimination. DGI [20] is the ﬁrst to introduce the InfoMax principle into GNN and augment the original
graph by simply shufﬂing node features. GMI [33] measures the mutual information between a center node and its local
patch from node-level and topology-level features. MVGRL [34] applies a graph diffusion network (GDN) to generate addi-
tional structural views. GCA [35] optimizes the uniform data augmentation schemes with the adaptive mechanism. HeCo
[36] considers two views of an HIN, including network schema and meta-path views, so as to capture local and high-level
structures simultaneously. CL4KT [37] introduces contrastive learning into KT and conducts the data augmentation on stu-
dents’ learning history sequence to reveal semantical similarity. But CL4KT cannot fuse the graph structure information and
capture the latent relations between nodes.

3.5. Adaptive Data Augmentation on HHN
Although contrastive learning has been widely applied in CV and NLP, it has rarely been explored in the graph ﬁeld. It is
still quite challenging to generate proper representation views for contrastive learning. Some existing methods construct
multiple views based on multi-hop neighbors from the perspective of inherent structure. While those models usually tend
to favor those observed connections, they may ignore the intrinsic (unobserved) relations underneath the input graph. The
impact of observed structures is magniﬁed in the layered information aggregation, which leads to the degree biases in learn-
ing representations and the vulnerability in facing noisy perturbations. To alleviate the above problem, we introduce a novel
data augmentation paradigm to generate the augmented view for HHN. Different from most of the augment-based methods,
which just remove nodes or edges equivalently and change the graph structure in a uniform manner, we propose an adaptive
scheme to adjust the dropout probability of each node according to its importance.

After obtaining the reﬁned question embeddings Hinh and Haug based on the inherent and augmented views, we integrate

In this section, we take several public datasets to evaluate the performance of our proposed model and some state-of-the-
art models. Particularly, two versions of the proposed HHN are investigated in the experiments, where HHN represents the
vanilla version (without self-supervised learning) and S2-HHN represents the self-supervised version. Furthermore, we con-
duct some ablation studies to verify the effectiveness of different components of HHN.
4.1. Datasets
We evaluate different models on several real-world knowledge tracing datasets, including ASSISTments20091,
ASSISTments20172, EdNet3 and JunyiAcademy4, which are widely used in the educational data mining ﬁeld to predict students’
performance. The statistics of the datasets are summarized in Table 1.
ASSISTments2009. This dataset was collected from ASSISTments online education platform during the school year 2009–
2010, which has two versions: Skill-builder and Non-Skill Builder. we select the updated Skill-builder version to conduct our
experiments and remove duplicated records. Besides, we remove those students who have less than 3 historical records.
ASSISTments2017. This dataset was collected from the same tutoring platform as ASSISTments2009 and was used in Data
Mining Competition 2017. It includes 11-year data spanning from middle schools for mathematics. We remove the data
recorded as ‘noskill’ in the skill ﬁeld.
EdNet. This dataset was collected from a multi-platform AI tutoring over 2 years with more than 780 K users in Korea,
which contains various features of students’ actions, such as the learning materials, the response, the time spent on solving
a given question or reading through experts’ commentary. Note that EdNet is composed of a total of 131441538 interactions
collected from 784,309 students, which is too large for training. So we randomly select 5000 students and their interactive
records to conduct experiments.
JunyiAcademy. The dataset contains the problem log and exercise-related information on the Junyi Academy. Besides, the
platform collects the annotations of exercise relationships for building models. Similarly, considering that the dataset is too
large, which contains 25925992 interactions collected from 247606 users, we randomly select 5000 users and their interac-
tive records.

4.2. Baselines
To verify the effectiveness of our method, we implement some state-of-the-art methods as baselines for evaluation.
DKT [7] is the ﬁrst method to introduce the deep neural network into the KT ﬁeld, which uses the long short term mem-
ory (LSTM) network to model the evolution of student state.
DKT+ [40] is an extension of traditional DKT method, which considers students’ historical interaction sequence and for-
getting behavior comprehensively.
DKVMN [9] utilizes the dynamic memory network and stores a student’s mastery of each skill by an external memory
matrix instead of a single hidden vector. The key matrix and value matrix are updated by read and write operations accord-
ing to each student’s historical sequences, which make the representation of his latent state more interpretable.
SAKT [11] introduces a self-attention mechanism to capture the relevance among interactive questions and focus on
those historical interactions that are highly relevant to the target question.
AKT [12] uses two-layer monotonic attention with Rasch embeddings, which integrates the exponential decay and a
context-aware relative distance measure, to model sequential interaction.
GIKT [13] adopts a graph structure to enrich the representation of questions and utilizes the GCN to capture question-skill
relations. Then, it applies an LSTM network to update the student’s latent knowledge state.
HawkesKT [24] takes the point process to explicitly model the temporal cross-skill impact and includes two components,
including mutual excitation and kernel function, to measure the degree of cross-effects.
CL4KT [37] applies the data augmentation method to generate two correlated views of a student’s history sequences and
introduces contrastive learning to learn discriminative representations for history sequences.

Compared with traditional graph-based methods, our model has two advantages: (1) it can capture different semantic
details from heterogeneous information and learn more informative representations for questions. By modeling various rela-
tions in HHN, the connections between distant questions can be well exploited. (2) it utilizes hypergraph topology to extract
the beyond-pairwise relations among questions. In real scenarios, similar questions are usually contained in one set. Tradi-
tional graph structure usually only supports pairwise relations between questions and is incapable of revealing such set-like
relations. The hypergraph structure, in which each hyperedge can connect multiple nodes, can naturally encode high-order
correlations.

4.5. Ablation Study
To verify the effectiveness of components of the proposed S2-HHN, we conduct some ablation studies to evaluate their
contributions in term of the AUC score. As the superiority of HHN results from the joint effects of the heterogeneous network
and the hypergraph structure, we develop several variants of HHN to validate their effectiveness for the ﬁnal performance.
 GCN [41]: adopts a graph convolutional network to aggregate neighbors’ features. Considering that it is designed for
homogeneous graphs, we test all the meta-paths for GCN and select the optimal performance for comparison.
 GAT [42]: adopts the attention mechanism to aggregate neighbors’ features on the homogeneous graph. Similarly, we test
all the meta-paths for GAT and show the optimal performance.
 HyperGCN: applies a hypergraph convolutional network [19] on the homogeneous hypergraph. We test all types of
hyperedges based on meta-paths and show the optimal performance.
 HeteroGCN: applies a graph convolutional network [41] on the heterogeneous graph. Here we aggregate neighbors’ fea-
tures on each meta-path and average them to generate the ﬁnal representation.
 HAN [26]: employs node-level and semantic-level attentions simultaneously on the heterogeneous graph, and assigns dif-
ferent importance to each node and meta-path.
 HHN-S: employs the proposed intra-graph attention on the homogeneous hypergraph. We test all types of hyperedges
and show the optimal performance.
As shown in Table 3, we see that HHN outperforms other variants.
1. Compared with the convolution methods (GCN, HyperGCN, HeteroGCN), which simply average over neighbors, attention
can adaptively adjust the weights between nodes and hyperedges and focus on important connective structures. By
assigning different importance to nodes and hyperedges, the proposed HHN can learn expressive representations for
questions.

Analysis of the prediction network. As mentioned before, the proposed HHN can be adapted to various downstream
prediction models. To demonstrate its suitability, we select several typical time-series methods as variants of the predic-
tion network and report their performance in Table 6.
 HHN + LSTM (DKT): supplements DKT with the proposed HHN. The reﬁned embeddings obtained from the HHN are then
fed into DKT [7] to generate ﬁnal prediction.
 HHN + S-GRU: sums the embeddings of questions and responses before RNN and then applies a simple GRU to predict a
student’s response.
 HHN + Attention (SAKT): adopts the self-attention mechanism proposed in SAKT [11] as the prediction network.
According to the results in Table 6, we can ﬁnd that HHN is capable of enhancing the downstream task performance, par-
ticularly improving DKT about 2.86% and 2.66%, SAKT about 2.21% and 3.15% on those two datasets. Such additional perfor-
mance gains also indicate the effectiveness of HHN from another perspective and conﬁrm that an adequate bottom
representation can improve the downstream prediction accuracy to some extent.
Meanwhile, we can observe that the traditional self-attention method yields the worst performance in all cases. A pos-
sible reason is that the memory decay has a signiﬁcant impact on students’ knowledge state. In KT, due to the long learning
cycle, the knowledge, which was well mastered before, may be forgotten now. Attending relative interactions simply over
long sequence is adverse to prediction.

 Dimension of initial question embeddings, D: The results are shown in Fig. 3a, where we can see that the model achieves
the best performance when D ¼ 256. The trend of raising ﬁrst and then dropping indicates that a lower dimension may be
inadequate to encode the semantic details while a higher dimension may cause additional redundancy for representation.
 Number of propagation layers of HHN, L: The results are shown in Fig. 3b. The model performs best when the number of
layers is set to 4. When L is less than 6, the ﬂuctuation of performance is not signiﬁcant. After that, the performance starts
to drop due to over-smoothing.
 Magnitude of self-supervised task, k: The results are shown in Fig. 3c. Note that the self-supervised task is removed when
k is set to 0. We can ﬁnd that the self-supervised task improves the performance of the prediction task consistently when
k is less than 2; a larger k would result in performance degradation. The reason lies in that a large weight would enforce
the model to focus on the auxiliary task excessively and the beneﬁts brought by the self-supervised framework could be
easily neutralized.
 Probability of dropout, qs;qk: The results are shown in Fig. 4. When the probability is not too large, the performance of S2-
HHN is relatively stable. If setting qk to a small value (0 or 0.1), we can ﬁnd that the performance keeps growing with the
increase of qs. While, for large qk (0.4 or 0.5), the performance declines precipitously. A high probability of dropout would
destroy the connective structures inevitably, which leads to the incapacity of learning the structural information and opti-
mizing the contrastive objective.
4.6. Visualization
For a more intuitive comparison, we conduct the task of visualization with several state-of-the-art methods and apply t-
SNE [43] to visualize the question embeddings. We randomly select ﬁve skills and the questions that cover them in ASSIST-
ments2017. As shown in Fig. 5a, DKT does not perform well. Due to Rasch embeddings, AKT forms a curve for questions with
the same skills. However, for those questions covering multiple skills, AKT tends to form isolated spots, which is adverse to
building representations. In GIKT, those questions covering different skills are likely mixed with each other. From Fig. 5d, we
see that our model divides nodes into ﬁve different groups, which demonstrates HHN can effectively keep intra-class sim-
ilarity and alleviate the semantic blending among questions with different skills to some extent.

HHN. We build a heterogeneous hypergraph to capture the heterogeneous and high-order relations among questions. With
the intra- and inter-graph attentions, our model can learn the importance of nodes and hyperedges. Moreover, to further
enhance the representation, we supplement the network with self-supervised task and generate the augmented view via
